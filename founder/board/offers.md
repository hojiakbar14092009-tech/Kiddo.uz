# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The dream outcome is clear and emotional.** A parent wants a 4–6-year-old who is ready for school: reading letters and counting, in the family's language. Few outcomes matter more to the person paying.
- **Low effort for the buyer.** The child plays alone, there is recorded voice in three languages, it works offline, and screens are ready in about 1 s on a slow phone. The parent sacrifices almost nothing.
- **A natural stack already exists.** Three alphabets, math, games, drawing studio, shorts, homework set by parents, and an attention helper (camera on only if a parent allows it). Most of these cost nothing extra to deliver once they are built.
- **Trust signals are built in.** No ads, no ad tracking, parental consent. Parents of young children pay for that.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nobody has picked the market yet.** Nothing is known about how many families there are, how many can spend about 25,000 so'm a month on an app, or how anyone finds the app ("not decided yet"). The best offer still fails if buyers are hard to reach or have no budget. Answers come from /founder-consumer (pain, money, where these parents gather) and /founder-competitors.
2. **There is no PRO offer yet, only a price.** The brief does not say what sits behind PRO. Without that, the parent's choice comes down to "free versus paid" and price, which makes the app a commodity. Nobody pays for what the free tier already gives them.
3. **Likelihood is unproven.** There is no evidence yet (no pilot children, no before/after, no testimonials, no guarantee) that a child actually learns the alphabet with Kiddo. Offers usually fail on missing proof, and a parent cannot see progress before paying.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which parent feels this pain most: Uzbek-medium, Russian-medium, or a family preparing a child for English? Pick one to lead with.
- What does a parent see after 7 days that proves it works (a progress report, "your child now knows 12 letters")?
- What exactly is in PRO, and why would a parent feel foolish saying no?
- What guarantee can you offer (for example "reads all letters in 60 days or a full refund")?
- How do the first 100 paying families arrive, and what does each one cost? (/founder-cfo for the unit economics, /founder-competitors for what rivals charge.)
- Could you charge double if the offer were a school-readiness programme instead of an app subscription?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Write the PRO stack around one outcome (for example "ready for school in letters and numbers"), with a visible parent progress report and a named programme.
2. Add a real, simple guarantee, plus a parent-facing result after the first week.
3. Run a small pilot with real families and collect proof (results, quotes) before spending on acquisition.
4. Return with market, competitor-price and CAC answers from /founder-consumer, /founder-competitors and /founder-cfo, and confirm a payment method that parents can use (Payme, Click or store billing).
